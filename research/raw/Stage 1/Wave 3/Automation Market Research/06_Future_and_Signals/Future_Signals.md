# Future signals: what to look forward to

## Signal 1 — From prompts to systems

Google’s 2026 agent-trends page frames the shift as agents orchestrating end-to-end workflows rather than answering isolated prompts. The practical implication is that orchestration, monitoring, permissions, and recovery become product requirements.

## Signal 2 — Protocol-led interoperability

MCP standardizes model access to tools and resources. A2A 1.0 standardizes communication between independent agents, including task state, streaming, push notifications, discovery, and authorization states. MCP is primarily agent-to-tool; A2A is agent-to-agent.

## Signal 3 — Durable agents

Temporal and LangGraph both emphasize stateful, long-running execution. The market is converging on agents that can survive crashes, wait for humans, retry safely, and resume from durable state.

## Signal 4 — Configurable autonomy

The likely enterprise pattern is not “fully autonomous.” It is policy-based autonomy: low-risk actions can run automatically, while high-impact actions require review, identity checks, or dual control.

## Signal 5 — Process intelligence becomes continuous

Process mining, event logs, traces, and agent telemetry will increasingly feed process redesign and evaluation. Automation will be measured by business outcomes, exception rates, auditability, and maintenance burden—not only task counts.

## Signal 6 — Hybrid estates

Companies will retain RPA for legacy UI, iPaaS for SaaS/API integration, durable runtimes for critical execution, and agent frameworks for interpretation and coordination.

## Watchlist

- Agent identity and delegated authorization.
- Signed capability cards and tool provenance.
- Evaluation of agent reliability in real workflows.
- Cost controls for model calls and retries.
- Standards for audit, policy, and human responsibility.
- Industry-specific automation products rather than generic agents.
