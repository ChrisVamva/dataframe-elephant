# Automation history and evolution

## 1. Industrial and workflow foundations

Automation began with mechanized production and later moved into information work. Workflow concepts became more prominent as organizations digitized records, approvals, and repetitive office procedures.

## 2. 1990s: agents, macros, screen scraping, workflow tools

Early software agents and multi-agent research explored autonomous coordination. Screen scraping, UI testing, macros, and workflow tools created practical automation where systems lacked APIs.

## 3. 2000s: RPA and visual automation

RPA combined UI interaction, workflow logic, OCR, and visual development. BPO and shared-service organizations adopted it for repetitive, rules-based work.

## 4. 2010s: cloud APIs, SaaS integration, iPaaS

Cloud applications made API-to-API integration more valuable. Zapier-like products democratized automation, while enterprise iPaaS platforms added governance and larger connector estates.

## 5. 2020s: durable orchestration and AI agents

Temporal-style durable execution addresses crashes, retries, timers, and long-running processes. LLM agents add flexible interpretation and planning, while LangGraph and similar runtimes add state, control, persistence, and human review.

## 6. Current synthesis

The market is moving from isolated task automation toward **process orchestration**: coordinating people, APIs, robots, models, and agents with evidence, policy, and recovery.

## Historical lesson

Each automation wave overpromised autonomy before solving maintenance, governance, exception handling, and accountability. The next durable winners will likely combine adaptability with strong operational controls.
