# Problems in automation

## Operational problems

- Fragile UI selectors and changing application interfaces.
- Silent failures, partial completion, and duplicate side effects.
- Poor retry semantics and unclear ownership of exceptions.
- Long-running processes that outlive sessions or workers.
- Automation sprawl with no inventory or lifecycle management.

## Economic problems

- Usage-based pricing becomes unpredictable at scale.
- Model calls, retries, storage, and human review are hidden costs.
- Low-code prototypes require expensive maintenance when they become critical.
- Enterprise contracts and implementation services obscure total cost.

## Governance problems

- Credentials and permissions are scattered across personal accounts.
- Human approval may be missing, ambiguous, or unaudited.
- Agent decisions can be difficult to explain or reproduce.
- Data may cross vendors, models, regions, or retention boundaries.
- Vendor lock-in can occur through proprietary connectors and workflow formats.

## Human problems

- Teams automate symptoms before understanding the process.
- Employees distrust opaque decisions or fear role displacement.
- No clear owner is assigned for agent or bot behavior.
- Poorly designed alerts create approval fatigue.

## Research implication

The central market gap is not “more automation.” It is **safe, observable, maintainable automation that fits the organization’s risk and operating model**.
