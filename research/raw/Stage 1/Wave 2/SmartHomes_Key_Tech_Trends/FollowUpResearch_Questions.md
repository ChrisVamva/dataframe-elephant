# Follow-up Research Questions

> Research backlog for extending the Smart Homes key technology trends synthesis.

## How to use this file

Prioritize questions that can change a product, investment, architecture, or policy decision. For each question, collect primary sources, label vendor claims separately from independent evidence, and record the date and geography of the evidence.

## Priority 1 — Resolve the biggest uncertainties

### Matter, Thread, and interoperability

- Which Matter 1.5/1.6 device types and features are supported consistently across Apple, Google, Amazon, and Samsung ecosystems?
- How often do optional Matter features, vendor extensions, or certification gaps create real-world incompatibility?
- What is the practical failure behavior when a Thread border router, home router, or cloud service goes offline?
- Can households migrate devices, routines, and permissions between ecosystems without rebuilding the home?
- How do Matter and Thread compare with legacy Zigbee, Z-Wave, and proprietary systems over a 5–10 year lifecycle?

### AI and contextual control

- What is the measured accuracy of AI-generated home automations, camera summaries, and natural-language device control?
- Which actions should always require confirmation, and which can safely be autonomous?
- How do leading assistants handle ambiguous users, multiple household members, guests, children, and adversarial voice commands?
- Which AI functions can run locally, and what latency, cost, privacy, and capability trade-offs result?
- Do AI-generated routines remain understandable, editable, testable, and portable by nontechnical users?

### Energy and sustainability

- What verified energy, demand-response, and bill savings are achieved by coordinated smart-home systems rather than isolated devices?
- Which combinations of HVAC, heat pumps, water heaters, batteries, solar, and EV charging produce the greatest flexibility?
- How do smart controls affect comfort, equipment lifetime, and peak-load emissions across different climates and building types?
- What utility tariffs, aggregators, and regulatory programs make residential flexibility financially attractive?
- Can Matter-based energy management interoperate reliably with utility and home-energy-management platforms?
## Priority 2 — Validate trust, safety, and lifecycle assumptions

### Security and privacy

- What minimum support period should consumers expect for locks, cameras, hubs, routers, appliances, and energy devices?
- How effective are network segmentation, automatic updates, multi-factor authentication, and secure commissioning in reducing household risk?
- Which smart-home data types are most sensitive: video, audio, occupancy, routines, energy, location, or inferred behavior?
- What happens to data, access, and device control when a vendor shuts down a cloud service or a household changes ownership?
- How are AI assistants audited for prompt injection, unauthorized actions, biometric misuse, and surveillance harms?
- Which security labels, certifications, and regulatory requirements provide useful information rather than compliance theater?

### Emerging product categories

- Are AI hubs, routers, and home-energy controllers becoming durable control points or interchangeable commodities?
- Which sensor categories create enough value to justify installation and maintenance costs?
- What evidence shows that AI-assisted cameras reduce false alerts or improve safety outcomes?
- Which automated comfort products work well in older homes and mixed, nonstandard HVAC systems?
- What new categories emerge when occupancy, energy, appliance, and security data are combined responsibly?

## Priority 3 — Investigate business and adoption dynamics

### Customer adoption

- What are the largest barriers after purchase: installation, configuration, maintenance, privacy, unreliable routines, or subscription costs?
- Which outcomes do customers value enough to pay for: convenience, safety, comfort, energy savings, aging-in-place support, or insurance benefits?
- How much setup can be delegated to installers, retailers, utilities, or AI assistants without reducing user understanding and control?
- How do households evaluate total cost of ownership when devices require hubs, subscriptions, batteries, connectivity, and replacement?

### Business and investor questions

- After Matter reduces basic interoperability friction, where can hardware companies still build defensible advantages?
- Which recurring-revenue models have the strongest retention without creating unacceptable lock-in or privacy concerns?
- How do support, cybersecurity, warranty, and software-update obligations affect lifetime gross margin?
- Which companies benefit most from the convergence of smart-home platforms and residential energy management?
- Are device counts, active homes, engagement, automation execution, energy savings, and subscription revenue being measured consistently?
- How will the Cyber Resilience Act and comparable rules change product design, documentation, vulnerability response, and market access?

## Suggested next research packages

1. **Interoperability test matrix** — compare commissioning, multi-admin, offline behavior, updates, and migration across major ecosystems.
2. **AI safety benchmark** — test intent interpretation, confirmation behavior, hallucinations, prompt injection, and recovery paths.
3. **Residential flexibility economics** — model HVAC, EV, battery, solar, and water-heater coordination under several tariffs and climates.
4. **Lifecycle security scorecard** — compare support periods, update mechanisms, certifications, privacy controls, and end-of-life policies.
5. **Adoption and willingness-to-pay study** — segment households by needs, technical confidence, housing type, and trust preferences.

## Research record fields

For each completed question, capture: question, date researched, geography, sources, evidence quality, key finding, unresolved uncertainty, and decision implication.
