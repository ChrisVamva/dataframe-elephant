## 2. Verbatim findings, platform by platform

### 2.1 Upwork — the conditional clause `[S01]`

`[documented fact]` The closest thing in Upwork's Terms to a worker-AI rule sits in §3.2 "Acting in a misleading or fraudulent way":

> "You can't misrepresent yourself, your experience, skills or professional qualifications, or that of others. This includes: lying about your experience, skills or professional qualifications / **using generative AI or other tools to substantially bolster your job proposals or work product if such use is restricted by your client or violates any third-party's rights** / passing off any part of someone else's profile or identity as your own…"

`[documented fact]` The same section requires honesty about who does the work: users can't "falsely claim one freelancer will do a job when another will actually do it – including submitting a proposal on behalf of a freelancer who can't or won't do the work."

`[inference]` The default rule is **permissive by construction**: AI use becomes misconduct only when (a) the *client* restricted it, or (b) third-party rights are violated. There is no platform-level disclosure duty and no platform-level ban — the permission level is set deal-by-deal by the client's instruction.

`[documented fact]` §2.4 "User Content and AI Tools" is about the *platform's* AI: opted-in users grant Upwork a licence to use job posts, proposals, communications, work product and reviews "to train generative AI models and tools," with opt-out via the site `[S01]`.

`[documented fact]` Upwork simultaneously operationalises AI as *supply*: the **AI Agent Playground Participation Terms (Beta) v1.2 (eff. 2025-07-29)** let "AI Partners" configure AI Agents to retrieve job posts via API and "submit outputs generated by their artificial intelligence agents … in response to Clients' project-specific job posts"; the Deliverable is submitted "without any edits or adjustments made by you," passed to clients "as is," and **feedback is "the sole form of payment"** for the AI Agent's output `[S02]`.

`[inference]` Upwork is the sharpest internal contradiction in the set: a human freelancer's AI-augmented proposal is misconduct-if-the-client-says-so `[S01]`, while an AI agent's unedited output has a first-class contractual pathway `[S02]` — the platform has rules for AI *as a participant* but none for AI *as a method*.

### 2.2 Fiverr — the explicit allow `[S03, S04, S05]`

`[documented fact]` Fiverr's Community Standards contain a dedicated section headed **"AI-generated content"**:

> "**Fiverr embraces new technology and allows the use of generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools to create content.** … AI tools may be used to assist freelancers in completing their work, but they are not a substitute for the freelancer's own skill, effort and judgment. Freelancers are accountable for the final work they deliver and must ensure that any AI-generated or AI-assisted work is high-quality, original, and meaningfully refined and customized to the client's specific requirements. **Misrepresenting the use of AI tools is a breach of trust**…"
>
> Prohibited: "AI tools may not be used to spread misinformation, create or distribute non-consensual content, create deepfakes in any form, including photo, video, or audio, or create fake accounts." Freelancers must hold "all necessary rights to the content they create," comply with tool ToS and applicable law, deliver no "generic, unmodified, or reused AI-generated output," and "own the final delivered work and be fully accountable for any error or inaccuracy, regardless of the use of AI tools." Sanctions: "order cancellation and a refund to the client"; removal and possible "permanent" suspension `[S03]`.

`[documented fact]` The Help Center guidance adds the labour-market default:

> "**Fiverr permits the use of AI across all service categories.** … **AI use is encouraged**, as long as it's: Responsible…; Compliant with laws and platform standards; **Used to support the freelancer's own skill and effort, not as a replacement for them**…"
>
> "**Freelancers must also honor a client's explicit request for non-AI work.** When a client specifies this preference, before or at the start of an order, freelancers are expected to disclose their intended workflow and align accordingly." Clients who don't want AI "must say so clearly, either before placing the order or immediately after." Dispute review weighs: prior communication of expectations; whether delivery is "free from clear mistakes or AI-generated hallucinations"; whether delivery "reflects the freelancer's own professional effort … rather than merely raw AI-generated output"; and communication quality. Sanction ladder: order cancelled + full refund → "the freelancer's account may be permanently suspended" for "misrepresentation of AI usage" `[S04]`.

`[documented fact]` Category-level rules add operational duties, e.g. voice work: no cloning "a known singer's voice" or "a celebrity's voice"; after delivery "all voice files provided by the client and any resulting voice model must be deleted" `[S05]`.

`[documented fact]` Fiverr's ToS body could not be inspected (client-rendered; archives contain only shells) — recorded **Blocked** `[S06]`; the binding status of the Community Standards is referenced by the help article ("Fiverr's Community Standards on AI-generated Content" `[S04]`), but the exact incorporation-by-reference clause was not verified in this audit `[S06]`.

`[inference]` Fiverr's text is the *de facto* model clause set: permission + quality floor + client opt-out + rights clearance + prohibited uses + an enforcement ladder — every element the silent platforms lack.

### 2.3 Toptal `[S07]`, Arc.dev `[S13]`, Codementor `[S14]`, Freelancermap `[S12]`

`[documented fact]` Full fetched texts of Toptal's Website T&Cs, Arc's ToS (mod. 2026-08-31), Codementor's ToS (mod. 2026-08-31) and freelancermap's Terms (state December 2018) contain **no** worker-AI provision under the audit keyword scan + manual review (2026-10-16) `[S07, S13, S14, S12]`.

- Toptal's T&Cs govern site use; the freelancer relationship runs on a talent-side agreement not published on the site — the audit's absence claim is scoped to the public document `[S07]`.
- freelancermap's Terms are a **December 2018** document `[S12]` — `[inference]` the silence is partly chronological: the instrument predates ChatGPT and has not been revised to address it (recheck trigger logged).

### 2.4 Freelancer.com `[S09]`, PeoplePerHour `[S10]`, Contra `[S08]`

`[documented fact]` **Freelancer.com's User Agreement (last modified 2025-06-05)** — full text scanned — contains zero occurrences of AI/artificial-intelligence/generative provisions `[S09]`. AI *categories* exist on the site, but the contract is silent `[S30]` (cf. §3 of this note).

`[documented fact]` **PeoplePerHour's T&Cs** mention machine learning once, and it is the platform's own model: §3.5.1 "Using historical data, our Data Science team have created machine learning models that try to predict which proposals a buyer will most likely want to accept" (proposal categorisation) `[S10]`. No worker-AI rule.

`[documented fact]` **Contra's Terms** have an AI section — but the subject is *content*, not labour: "Users are responsible for ensuring their use of AI generated content does not infringe on any third party rights…"; "AI models generate content that may sometimes contain unintended biases, incorrect, incomplete or offensive information. Contra does not guarantee the accuracy… You should not rely on such AI generated content as a sole source of truth… including human review as appropriate." The same Terms govern "Indy AI," Contra's own assistant/search product `[S08]`.

`[inference]` Contra regulates AI the way a platform regulates a *feature* (disclaimers, user responsibility), not the way it regulates *work* (disclosure, quality floor, client opt-out): there is no clause about delivering AI-assisted work to a client.

### 2.5 Malt `[S11]`, Braintrust `[S15–S18]`, Botpool `[S19, S20]`

`[documented fact]` **Malt's Community T&Cs (in force 2026-04-16)**: §5.4 "Artificial intelligence-based features" — user data "may be used for the training, validation and improvement of Malt's internal machine learning models"; §12.8 "Artificial intelligence" — Malt "is unable to guarantee … the accuracy, completeness, impartiality or relevance of the results generated by the integrated tools." Both are platform-AI clauses; the freelancer's use of AI in delivery is unaddressed `[S11]`.

`[documented fact]` **Braintrust** publishes the set's only dedicated **Responsible AI Policy** (Freelance Labs Inc., last updated 2026-05-01) — but its scope is the platform's own recruiting product: it "applies to artificial intelligence systems developed, operated, or made available by Freelance Labs, including Braintrust AIR and related AI-assisted recruiting, screening, interviewing, and candidate evaluation workflows," with human-oversight duties, a NYC Local Law 144 bias audit, EEOC/NIST/OECD alignment, and an EU AI Act readiness program "targeting completion of applicable compliance activities by August 1, 2026" `[S18]`. The worker-facing layer — Site Terms `[S15]`, Talent Terms `[S16]`, Network Standards & Code of Conduct `[S17]` — contains no AI-work item in its prohibited-conduct lists `[S15–S17]`.

`[inference]` Braintrust demonstrates the asymmetry at the heart of the audit: the platform writes careful rules for *its own AI touching workers* (candidates being screened), and none for *workers using AI touching clients*.

`[documented fact]` **Botpool** — the AI-native niche marketplace proposed in the FRE scans — has short Terms of Service (15 sections: definitions, accounts, payments, IP, disclaimers, governing law) with **no AI-work clause**; AI appears only as the marketplace's subject matter in the FAQ ("browse categories to find the AI service you need"; "vetted before listing") `[S19, S20]`.

`[inference]` The niche finding is counterintuitive and useful for the article: a marketplace built *entirely* for AI work is as silent on worker-AI rules as the generalists — being AI-native did not produce an AI-work policy.

## 3. The positioning/rule gap (summary row for RQ3 — detail in `../03_Demand_and_Positioning/Positioning.md`)

`[documented fact]` All twelve platforms market AI work in taxonomy or product form `[S30]`; **1/12** explicitly regulates worker AI use `[S03, S04]`.

## 4. What generic clauses already cover (the fallback law)

`[documented fact]` Where AI-work rules are absent, platforms fall back on: misrepresentation/honesty clauses (Upwork §3.2 `[S01]`, Braintrust profile-truth rules `[S15]`), IP/DMCA provisions (Upwork, Braintrust, Botpool `[S01, S15, S19]`), and feedback-system manipulation bans (Braintrust `[S15]`).

`[inference]` These can reach *fraudulent* AI use (fake identity via AI imagery — Fiverr's standards ban this explicitly `[S03]`) but cannot reach the ordinary dispute: undetectable, competent, AI-assisted delivery that the client never restricted. The default leaves **the disclosure question to contract between two private parties**, on platforms that mostly don't surface it — the gap `05_Clause_Design/` addresses.
