# Synthesis — Nobody Bans AI-Assisted Work (Wave 8)

_Date: 2026-10-16. Answers to RQ1–RQ5 with falsifiers. Every claim cites `[S##]` from `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`. Claim types: `[documented fact]` / `[reported signal]` / `[inference]`._

## Central finding

**The freelance AI economy runs on a rule that was never written.** Auditing the public governing documents of all12 FRE-scan platforms on 2026-10-16: **0 ban** AI-assisted work, **1 explicitly allows it** (Fiverr, with a quality floor, client opt-out, rights clearance, harms list and sanction ladder) `[S03, S04]`, **2 address it only indirectly** (Upwork's client-restriction trigger `[S01]`; Contra's AI-content disclaimers `[S08]`), **3 regulate only their own AI** (PPH `[S10]`, Malt `[S11]`, Braintrust `[S18]`), and **6 are silent** `[S07, S09, S12–S14, S19]`. Meanwhile **all12 sell AI work** in categories, trending skills or AI-branded products `[S30]`, and the two freshest demand signals — Upwork's own FFWI (`+34%` hourly premium for AI work, `[S26]`) and a peer-reviewed JEBO study (substitutable skills −20–50%, chatbot dev ≈×3, `[S28]`) — show a segment too large to leave ungoverned. Where the rule exists elsewhere — Steam, Etsy, Google Play, FTC, EU AI Act — it follows one pattern: **allow, declare, own the output, punish harms** `[S21–S25]`. The deliverable clause set (`../05_Clause_Design/`) transfers that pattern to labour, anchored on Upwork's honesty clause extended to agents `[S01, S02]`.

The sharpest single contradiction: **Upwork gives AI agents a contract** — AI Agent Playground terms where agents submit unedited deliverables and feedback is "the sole form of payment" `[S02]` — **while a human's AI-assisted proposal is misconduct only if the client forbade it** `[S01]`. Platforms have rules for AI *as a participant*; none for AI *as a method*.

## RQ1 — Ban / allow / silence

- 0/12 ban · 1/12 explicit-allow (Fiverr `[S03, S04]`) · 2/12 conditional-indirect (Upwork `[S01]`, Contra `[S08]`) · 3/12 platform-own-AI-only (`[S10, S11, S18]`) · 6/12 silent (`[S07, S09, S12–S14, S19]`).
- Fallback law where silent: generic misconduct clauses (misrepresentation, IP, fake reviews) — reach fraudulent AI use, not ordinary undisclosed AI-assisted delivery `[inference; S01, S15, S19]`.
- Niche paradox: Botpool, an AI-only marketplace, is silent `[S19, S20]`.
- Full per-platform rows + verbatim quotes: `../02_Platform_Policy_Audit/Audit.md`.

## RQ2 — Where the rules live

- The only full rule-set sits outside the binding Terms: Fiverr's Community Standards + two Help Center articles; the ToS body is **Blocked** (client-rendered) and the incorporation chain unverified `[S03, S04, S06]`.
- Upwork's text exists *inside* the Terms but hidden as a bullet of the fraud clause `[S01]`.
- Six platforms give no answer at any layer. **Discoverability fails even where existence doesn't** `[inference]`.

## RQ3 — Positioning gap

- Marketing is universal (AI category trees, "AI-powered" branding, AI Gig Work product) `[S30]`; rules are exceptional (1/12) `[S03, S04]`.
- Two demand realities, both non-trivial: company research reports +34% hourly premium for AI work and +90% gen-AI contract-start growth with −13% per-contract earnings `[S26]`; independent JEBO evidence shows a substitution shock in writing/translation and a specialization shift `[S28]`.
- **No platform discloses the AI-assisted share of delivered work** — the buyer-relevant number does not exist publicly `[inference; absence across audit set]`.

## RQ4 — Outside context

- Disclosure traditions are mature and operable: Steam (declare pre-/live-generated; review like non-AI content; efficiency tooling out of scope) `[S21]`, Etsy (one-field listing disclosure) `[S22]`, Google Play (developer owns outputs) `[S23]`.
- Regulators already reached AI output provenance: FTC — "AI-generated reviews are covered by the final rule" `[S24]`; EU AI Act Art. 50 — machine-readable marking + deepfake disclosure `[S25]`.
- Every platform in the set *has* drafted AI rules — for its own AI (training licences, accuracy disclaimers, a full Responsible AI Policy at Braintrust) `[S01, S11, S18]`. The missing clause is about the other party's conduct `[inference]`.
- Enforcement opacity is total: no platform publishes AI-related enforcement data `[absence; 2026-10-16]`.
