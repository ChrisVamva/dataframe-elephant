# Demand and Positioning — Platforms Selling AI While Regulating It Last

_Date: 2026-10-16. Every claim cites `[S##]`. Claim types: `[documented fact]` / `[reported signal]` / `[inference]`. Companion to `../02_Platform_Policy_Audit/Audit.md` (RQ3)._

## 1. The gap, stated

`[documented fact]` All 12 audited platforms market AI work in their taxonomy or product surface `[S30]`; **1 of 12** explicitly regulates the freelancer's use of AI `[S03, S04]`.

`[inference]` The ordering is consistent: **discovery-side AI marketing is standard; rules-side AI governance is the exception.** AI appears where it attracts supply and demand (categories, trending skills, "AI-powered" branding) before it appears where it would constrain either.

## 3. Independent evidence: what happened to the demand mix

`[documented fact]` Teutloff et al. (JEBO, 2025) partitioned job postings from "a leading online freelancing platform" into 116 skill clusters and classify them LLM-substitutable/complementary/unaffected: after ChatGPT's launch, demand **fell 20–50%** for substitutable clusters (writing, translation; sharpest for 1–3-week jobs); ML programming **+24%**; AI-powered chatbot development **nearly tripled**; **novice-worker demand declined in general** `[S28]`.

`[documented fact]` A 2026 arXiv preprint (Imteyaz et al.) frames freelance knowledge workers as lacking "the organizational training, mentorship, and infrastructure available to traditional employees," with gen-AI tools as self-directed upskilling `[S29]` (preprint, not peer-reviewed).

`[inference]` Together with the platform data `[S26]`, the independent picture is: AI-assisted work is now a **structural, growing segment** of freelance demand — and the segment most exposed to substitution is the one where buyers have the least contractual protection from undisclosed AI use (short-cycle writing/translation work `[S28]`).

## 4. The gap thesis (for the article)

`[inference]` The three evidence strands compose the article's central tension:

1. **Supply side:** platforms brand and merchandise AI work across every audited marketplace `[S30]`, and one explicitly "encourages" AI use `[S04]`.
2. **Demand side:** independent research shows buyers of substitutable skills already absorbed a 20–50% demand shock `[S28]`, while platform research sells AI as a premium skill `[S26]`.
3. **Rule side:** 6 of 12 platforms say nothing, 3 regulate only their own AI, and only 1 publishes a worker-AI clause `[S01–S19]`.

`[inference]` The mismatch is the story: **the AI-labour market is being built on a governance vacuum**, filled ad hoc by client instructions (Upwork's "if such use is restricted by your client" `[S01]`) and post-hoc dispute review (Fiverr `[S04]`).

## 5. Limits

- Category trees evidence *marketing structure*, not volume or revenue `[S30]`.
- Upwork figures are company research (C); JEBO findings are platform-composite and predate 2025 marketplace changes `[S26, S28]`.
- FRE probe counts are internal one-day observations `[S31]`.
- No platform discloses AI-assisted share of delivered work — the single most relevant number for this article does not exist publicly `[inference; absence of disclosure observed across audit set 2026-10-16]`.
