# Regimes — How Law Defines and Verifies Origin

_Covers RQ1 (regime side). Every claim cites `[S##]` from `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`. Research pass of 2026-10-06. Status as of Oct 2026 throughout._

## 1. Semiconductor export controls (US / allies / China)

### 1.1 US BIS timeline (2022–2026)

- **Oct 7, 2022** — two interim final rules restrict China's access to advanced computing chips, supercomputing items, and semiconductor manufacturing equipment; new ECCN 3A090 and end-use controls at EAR §744.23 (published 87 FR 62186, compliance Oct 21 2022) [S01].
- **Oct 17, 2023** — controls expanded (performance-density parameters, additional country coverage); exact FR citations not verified this session (open item).
- **Dec 2, 2024** — controls on 24 types of SME, 3 software tool types, and HBM; ~140 Chinese entities added to the Entity List; FDP-rule and de minimis expansions published Dec 5 2024 [S02].
- **Jan 13, 2025** — AI Diffusion Rule (worldwide 3-tier control of AI model weights and advanced computing ICs; compliance May 15 2025) — **rescinded May 13, 2025** days before taking effect, replacement promised [S05].
- **Sep 29, 2025** — **Affiliates Rule**: any entity ≥50% owned (directly/indirectly, aggregated) by Entity List/MEU parties inherits their restrictions without being listed — modeled on OFAC's 50% rule; targets evasion via layered ownership [S03].
- **Jan 15, 2026** — license-review policy for China/Macau revised: presumption of denial replaced by **case-by-case review** for certain AI chips (H200/MI325X-class, with security conditions) [S06].
- Enforcement: BIS FY2025 civil penalties reported ~$324M vs ~$16M FY2024 (secondary citation, verify against BIS annual report) [S13].

### 1.2 Allied alignment

- Netherlands: export licensing for ASML's most advanced immersion DUV (NXT:2000i and above) from Sep 2023; partial revocation of a 2023 shipment license (NXT:2050i/2100i) on Jan 1 2024; EUV blocked since 2019 [S08].
- Japan: METI ordinance covering 23 SME types in six categories, effective **Jul 23, 2023**, individual licenses required [S09].
- Sep–Oct 2025 Nexperia episode: Dutch invocation of the Goods Availability Act against a Chinese-owned (Wingtech) chipmaker on the US Entity List; China counter-banned Nexperia China-made chip exports — a jurisdictional fight over "which country's chips are these" [S72].

### 1.3 China's mineral export controls and current status

- Licensing: gallium/germanium (Jul 3 2023, effective Aug 1 2023); graphite (Dec 1 2023); antimony (Sep 2024) [S11, S12].
- **Dec 3, 2024**: export **ban to the US** of gallium, germanium, antimony, superhard materials (MOFCOM Notice 2024 No. 46) [S11].
- **Apr 4, 2025**: seven medium/heavy rare earths (Sm, Gd, Tb, Dy, Lu, Sc, Y) incl. magnets controlled; US-bound yttrium/terbium/scandium/dysprosium exports effectively halted after April 2025 [S12].
- **Oct 9, 2025** (Announcement No. 61/62): 12 controlled REEs total; controls extended to REE **technologies and equipment**; a **0.1% value de minimis** for foreign-made items containing Chinese-origin rare earths; extraterritorial licensing for foreign-made products made with Chinese REE technology/equipment (a Chinese FDP analog); effective Dec 1 2025 [S07].
- **Nov 2025 truce**: China suspended No. 61/62 from Nov 7/10 2025 to **Nov 10, 2026** and issued general licenses for REE/Ga/Ge/Sb/graphite [S10]. USGS notes a one-year suspension of the gallium ban to the US [S61]. The April 2025 seven-element controls and the 2023 Ga/Ge licensing were **not** reversed [S10].
- License approvals: ~25% of auto-supplier applications approved as of Jun 2025 (journalistic); MOFCOM claimed all compliant civilian applications approved by Dec 5 2025; no official overall rate published (open item) [S10].

## 2. Critical-minerals regimes

- **EU CRMA** (Reg (EU) 2024/1252): 2030 benchmarks — ≥10% extraction, ≥40% processing, ≥25% recycling of EU consumption; diversification cap: no third country >65% of consumption of any strategic raw material; strategic projects get permitting time limits (15/27 months) [S14]. First 47 intra-EU strategic projects Mar 25 2025; 13 third-country projects Jun 4 2025 [S15].
- **US side**: MP Materials–DoD deal (Jul 10 2025): $400M preferred stock + warrants making DoD the largest shareholder, 10-year $110/kg NdPr price floor — the state as direct buyer-of-origin [S66]. Pentagon planned ~$1B critical-mineral purchases via DLA (up to $500M cobalt, $245M antimony, $100M tantalum) in Oct 2025; CRS documents a **$13.5B stockpile gap** [S67].
- Concentration context that makes these regimes binding: China is the dominant refiner for 19 of 20 IEA-tracked minerals (~70% average share, rising to 72% in 2025) [S59, S60]; China ~99% of primary gallium [S61]; ~48% antimony, ~79–80% tungsten mine production [S62].

## 3. Forced-labour import rules

- **UFLPA** (US, since Jun 21 2022): **rebuttable presumption** that goods made wholly or in part with XUAR inputs are forbidden — importer bears the burden of "clear and convincing evidence" plus complete supply-chain tracing to raw-material level [S17]. Enforcement (CBP dashboard): ~428 shipments/month in 2024 (~$1.4–1.5B value); FY2025 ~7,325 shipments stopped, +>50% YoY; Entity List grew +37 to **144 entities** (Jan 14 2025) [S19]; H1 2025 ~6,636 detentions with **automotive at ~86%** (vs 4% in 2024) as enforcement moved from apparel/solar into electronics, autos, battery chains [S20].
- **EU Forced Labour Regulation** (Reg (EU) 2024/3015 — note: not 2024/1765): in force Dec 13 2024, **applies from Dec 14, 2027**; bans placing/making available/exporting products made with forced labour at any supply-chain stage. **No rebuttable presumption — the investigating authority bears the burden of proof**, with an adverse-inference mechanism if the operator refuses cooperation; penalties set nationally (no EU-wide fine) [S18].
- The two regimes encode opposite verification philosophies: the US shifts burden onto the importer (who must *prove a negative* about origin); the EU keeps burden on the state (who must *prove forced labour* with limited investigative tools). Neither requires a physical origin witness.

## 4. Conflict-minerals regimes

- **OECD Due Diligence Guidance** (3rd ed 2016, updated 2022): the five-step framework (management systems → risk identification → response strategy → independent smelter/refiner audits → annual reporting) underpins effectively every 3TG/cobalt regime worldwide [S21]. Its evidence model is **attestation-based**: risks are identified and mitigated through paper trails and audits, with no physical binding of ore to mine.
- **EU Regulation 2017/821**: importer due-diligence obligations for 3TG since Jan 1 2021, aligned to the OECD framework [S22].
- Dodd-Frank §1502 (US SEC disclosure) remains the lineage ancestor of all of these; its disclosure-only model demonstrated that reporting without verification produces paperwork, not provenance (inference from the scheme-design history).

## 5. How each regime determines "origin" — the four jurisdiction tests

1. **Content/de minimis**: foreign-made items subject to the EAR when controlled US content exceeds thresholds — 25% general, 10% sensitive destinations, 0% for certain advanced-computing items to China/Macau [S16]. China's mirror: 0.1% de minimis for foreign items with Chinese REE content [S07].
2. **Production lineage (FDP rules)**: items that are the direct product of US-origin technology/software, or made in a plant that is itself such a product, are covered even when wholly produced abroad [S04].
3. **Ownership**: the 50% Affiliates Rule [S03]; IRA FEOC ownership thresholds function similarly for clean-energy credits.
4. **Substantial transformation** (customs/sanctions): origin = where the last substantial transformation occurred — the OFAC diamond FAQ's operative concept [S25]; Commerce's solar circumvention determinations asked whether third-country processing was "minor or insignificant" [S31].

**Assessment (inference):** all four tests are computable from *documents* (declarations, BOMs, corporate registries), never from the *physical object*. Every regime degrades to attestation at the moment of the first physical hand-off. That is the structural opening for provenance laundering documented in `../03_Laundering_Cases/`.
