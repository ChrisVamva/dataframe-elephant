# Field-Reversed Configuration (FRC)

_State as of 2026-10-02. Citations: `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`._

## How it works

A compact toroid with purely poloidal field and no toroidal field coils — a self-organized "smoke ring" of plasma confined in a linear device. High beta (plasma pressure ≈ magnetic pressure) means compact machines. FRCs can be translated and compressed; in Helion's scheme two FRCs collide and are compressed by pulsed magnets to induce a fusion pulse with direct electricity recovery via induction.

## Maturity & best results (company claims — treat as [C])

- **Helion**: Feb 13, 2026 — announced Polaris (7th-generation device) became the **first private fusion machine operating on D–T fuel**, achieving fusion at ~150M °C (company temperature record) [S25] [S48]. No net electricity announced; no peer-reviewed net-gain result. Raised Series G of $465M (Jun 2026, ~$15.5B valuation); building the Orion plant at Malaga, WA under the Microsoft ≥50 MW / 2028 PPA — the sector's most-watched and most-questioned deadline [S25] [S44].
- **TAE**: 2025 — peer-reviewed first formation of an FRC plasma using only neutral beam injection (Nature Communications); sixth-generation machine "Copernicus" in development; partnership with UKAEA on neutral beams (Dec 2025). **Dec 18, 2025: announced >$6B all-stock merger with Trump Media (DJT)**, pending as of Oct 2026, with a claimed 50 MWe "Da Vinci" plant to start siting in 2026 [S26].

## Advantages / disadvantages

- **+** Compact, high-beta, linear geometry (easy maintenance/shielding); natural fit for pulsed direct-electric conversion (Helion) and aneutronic ambitions (TAE p–¹¹B).
- **−** FRC stability and confinement at reactor scale is the core open physics question; pulsed-power electronics fatigue; both companies' timelines (2028 grid power; early-2030s commercial) lack independent validation [S44] [S25].

## Representative devices

Polaris (Helion), Norman/Copernicus (TAE), PFRC (Princeton Fusion Systems).
