# Stellarator

_State as of 2026-10-02. Citations: `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`._

## How it works

Confinement is produced entirely by external, precisely shaped twisted coils — no plasma current. This yields intrinsically steady-state, disruption-free operation at the cost of very complex coil geometry, now tractable via HTS magnets and advanced manufacturing/computational optimization.

## Maturity & best results

- W7-X (IPP Greifswald, the flagship): May 22, 2025 — world-record plasma energy turnover of **1.8 GJ over 360 s** (beating its own Feb 2023 record of 1.3 GJ/480 s) [S09] [S10]; stellarator **triple-product record** with 43 s of high-performance plasma [S08]. Down ~1 year for upgrades before the next campaign [S10].
- Private momentum (2025–2026):
  - **Proxima Fusion** (W7-X spinout): €411M round (Jul/Sep 2026) — Europe's largest private fusion round; agreement for a first stellarator plant in Bavaria [S41].
  - **Thea Energy** (planar-coil stellarator): $100M Series B (May 2026); first DOE Milestone awardee to get DOE certification for its Helios pilot plant [S42].
  - **Type One Energy**: TVA cooperation for a 350 MW plant at the former Bull Run coal site (TN); received the first US state fusion operating license (Tennessee, Sep 2026) [S23].
  - Renaissance Fusion (France): stellarator + liquid-metal blanket concept [S41 context].

## Advantages / disadvantages

- **+** Steady-state, disruption-free; no current drive needed; W7-X has validated the 3D-optimization physics that was the concept's core risk.
- **−** Historically lower confinement than optimized tokamaks (gap closing but not closed); extremely complex coils and manufacturing; smaller reactor-scale engineering database (divertor, alpha-particle confinement).

## Representative devices

W7-X (Germany), LHD (Japan), HSX (Wisconsin), TJ-II (Spain); private: Proxima Fusion, Thea Energy, Type One Energy, Renaissance Fusion.
