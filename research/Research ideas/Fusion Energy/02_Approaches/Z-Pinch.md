# Z-Pinch (incl. sheared-flow Z-pinch and MagLIF)

_State as of 2026-10-02. Citations: `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`._

## How it works

A huge axial current through a plasma produces an azimuthal magnetic field that compresses ("pinches") it. Classic pinches fail to kink/sausage instabilities; Zap Energy's **sheared-flow** variant stabilizes them with axial flow velocity shear. Sandia's **MagLIF** (magnetized liner inertial fusion) pre-magnetizes fuel inside a metal liner and implodes it with Z's megaampere current — a hybrid of pinch and inertial confinement.

## Maturity & best results

- **Zap Energy**: Apr 2025 — 1–3 keV electron temperatures in sheared-flow-stabilized pinches; peer-reviewed sustained neutron production (FuZE). Next machine **Century** — first-of-a-kind repetitive (~12 pulses/min) Z-pinch platform with liquid-metal cooling — became operational 2025 [S28]. Received a DOE grant and DOE approval of its pre-conceptual power-plant design; total funding ~$327M (no major new round found in 2025–26).
- **Sandia Z / MagLIF**: benchmark >10¹³ DD neutrons per shot (record ~2020); 2025 work on auto-magnetizing liners; no new record yield found for 2025–26.

## Advantages / disadvantages

- **+** No superconducting magnets, no neutral beams (Zap) — linear, compact, cheap to iterate; liquid-metal wall compatibility (Century).
- **−** Pulsed electrodes/liner survival; stability margin at higher current is extrapolated, not proven; net-gain path for a pure pinch undemonstrated; MagLIF is single-shot with slow rep-rate.

## Representative devices

FuZE/Century (Zap Energy), Z machine (Sandia). Note: UK's Pulsar Fusion is a **space-propulsion** venture (Sunbird fusion rocket, first plasma Mar 2026), not a power company.
