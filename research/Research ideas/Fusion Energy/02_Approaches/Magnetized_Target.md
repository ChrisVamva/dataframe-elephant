# Magnetized Target Fusion (MTF)

_State as of 2026-10-02. Citations: `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`._

## How it works

Hybrid of magnetic and inertial confinement: a magnetized plasma (often an FRC-like toroid) is formed, then compressed rapidly — mechanically via pistons or electromagnetically — to fusion conditions on a millisecond timescale, intermediate between magnetic (slow) and inertial (fast) confinement.

## Maturity & current status (General Fusion is the field)

- LM26 ("Lawson Machine 26", Vancouver): magnetized plasma successfully formed (Mar 2025, peer-reviewed confinement result); plasma heated to ~8.4M °C (0.72 keV), en route to 1 keV and ultimately 10 keV milestones [S29].
- **2025 funding crisis**: ~25% of staff laid off (May 2025) after a shortfall; emergency raises (C$20M bridge, C$51.5M Dec 2025) [S29].
- **Jul 2026: became the first publicly listed pure-play fusion company** — Nasdaq via SPAC merger with Spring Valley Acquisition Corp. III, ticker **GFUZ**; market cap ~$404M by Sep 2026, well below the ~$1B deal valuation [S29].

## Advantages / disadvantages

- **+** Simpler, cheaper machine architecture than tokamaks; liquid-metal-compatible compression chamber concept; compression physics demonstrated incrementally.
- **−** Unproven at power-plant scale; requires simultaneous success of magnetization, compression timing, and liner integrity; LM26 is far from net energy; the 2025 near-death experience illustrates the capital-intensity risk of the whole sector [S29].

## Representative devices

LM26 (General Fusion); historical MTF work at Los Alamos / Air Force Research Lab.
