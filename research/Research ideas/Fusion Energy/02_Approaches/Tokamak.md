# Tokamak

_State as of 2026-10-02. Citations: `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`._

## How it works

A toroidal (doughnut-shaped) vessel uses strong magnetic coils to confine a >100-million-°C plasma. A central solenoid drives a large plasma current that creates the poloidal field; toroidal + poloidal fields combine into the helical confinement. Spherical tokamaks compress the geometry into a cored-apple shape with a slender central column — high magnetic efficiency at compact size (MAST-U, STEP, Tokamak Energy).

## Maturity & best results

- **Most mature approach** — largest physics database, and the only approach with a reactor-scale multinational project (ITER) under assembly [S12].
- Energy record: JET 69 MJ over ~5.2 s (D–T, Oct 2023); Q≈0.33 — net energy not yet achieved in a tokamak [S04].
- SPARC (CFS, HTS REBCO magnets): >75% assembled; first plasma targeted 2026, independent analysis suggests 2027; designed for Q>1 [S24] [S50].
- Long-pulse: EAST 1,066 s H-mode (Jan 2025); KSTAR 48 s at 100M °C (2024) [S06] [S05].
- MAST-U: first use of 3D magnetic coils (RMP) to suppress ELM instabilities (Oct 2025); Super-X divertor demonstrated ~10× exhaust-heat reduction [S20].

## Advantages / disadvantages

- **+** Deepest physics and confinement-scaling knowledge; industrial supply chain forming around HTS magnets; spherical variant is compact.
- **−** Pulsed or current-drive-limited operation; disruptions and ELMs; divertor heat loads; ITER-class machines large, slow, expensive — schedule risk demonstrated by the 2024 rebaseline (research ops ~2033–34, D–T ~2039) [S13].

## Representative devices

Public: ITER, JET (decommissioned 2024), EAST, KSTAR, JT-60SA, DIII-D, MAST-U, NSTX-U (still under repair), SPARC (private), STEP. Private: Commonwealth Fusion Systems (SPARC/ARC), Tokamak Energy (ST40/ST80-HTS).
