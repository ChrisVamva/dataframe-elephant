# Open Challenges

_Core deliverable for RQ4. State as of 2026-10-02. Citations: `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`._

## 1. Materials (14 MeV neutron environment)

- No structural material is qualified for the combined 14 MeV neutron flux, heat, and helium embrittlement of a D–T power plant. Baseline candidates: reduced-activation ferritic-martensitic steels (Eurofer97) + tungsten plasma-facing components.
- Qualification facility IFMIF-DONES (Granada, Spain) is only in construction of its main accelerator building (works underway 2026) — DEMO-relevant materials data cannot exist before the mid-2030s at best. No firm first-beam date confirmed.
- Divertors: ITER's tungsten monoblocks rated ~10–20 MW/m² steady-state [S12].

## 2. Tritium fuel cycle

- **No integrated breeding blanket has ever operated in a fusion neutron environment.** ITER will test blanket mockups, not a self-sufficient cycle [S12] [S49].
- World civilian tritium stock: tens of kg, mostly from CANDU reactors (Ontario, Korea); a single D–T plant needs several kg startup inventory and consumes a meaningful share of world supply per year [S49].
- Key metric: tritium breeding ratio (TBR) ≥ required TBR (Sawan & Abdou 2006); MIT's BABY 14MeV experiment is producing the first direct TBR-model validation data [S49].
- **Lithium-6 chokepoint**: only Russia and China actively produce enriched Li-6 (US COLEX shut down decades ago); blankets typically need 30–90% Li-6 enrichment [S33].

## 3. Heat exhaust / divertor

- Detached (radiative) divertor operation is the required baseline mode but is hard to control in real time. Europe's DTT facility exists specifically to test advanced divertors (Super-X etc.) beyond ITER's capability; UKAEA's Super-X demonstrated ~10× exhaust-heat reduction on MAST-U [S20]; PPPL identified a new plasma-escape mechanism (Jun 2024) as a potential mitigation route.

## 4. From scientific to engineering breakeven

- NIF: target gain 4.13 (Apr 2025) but engineering gain ~0.02 — laser wall-plug efficiency ~0.5%, ~1 shot/day. A plant needs ~100× total gain (target gain × driver efficiency × thermal conversion × availability) and Hz-class rep-rates [S01] [S03].
- Tokamaks: recirculating power (current drive, cryogenics) consumes a large fraction of gross output; competitive plants need Q_plasma ≳ 10–30.

## 5. Economics

- Point estimates span ~$50–150+/MWh; MIT's Nicholas et al. (2021) optimistic FOAK estimate ~$91/MWh; capital costs $4,000–8,000/kWe (Lindley et al. 2023). Early fusion is generally projected **not LCOE-competitive** against SMRs ($40–60) or renewables+storage ($65–90); the value case is firm, high-capacity-factor power [S34].
- Skeptical literature: Jassby (Bulletin, 2017) on internal power consumption and true costs [S35]; Clean Air Task Force costing standard (arXiv, Feb 2026) exists to discipline compact-fusion cost claims.

## 6. Regulation (moving faster than engineering)

- **US**: NRC chose the byproduct-materials framework (10 CFR Part 30), not the reactor framework (Part 50) — May 2023; rulemaking targeted for completion ~2027 [S36]. First state license: Tennessee → Type One Energy, Sep 2026 [S23].
- **UK**: fusion regulated outside nuclear site licensing (Energy Act 2023); National Policy Statement response Jul 2025 — no population-density restriction, facilities <50 MWe exempt from the NPS consent route [S37]; STEP DCO submission due Mar 2029 [S19].
- **Canada**: CNSC consulting on fusion regulatory readiness (DIS-25-01, Aug 2025) [S38].

## 7. Supply chain & workforce

- **REBCO/HTS tape**: a commercial reactor needs thousands of km; global capacity (Faraday Factory, Fujikura, SuperPower, Shanghai Superconductor, …) is far below projected demand — a recognized scaling bottleneck, geopolitically concentrated.
- **Workforce**: industry ~9,000 employees (FIA 2025); large projected growth needs [S30] [S31].

## Open question

- Which is the true critical path — materials (IFMIF-DONES timescale), tritium self-sufficiency (blankets + Li-6 supply), or economics (LCOE vs firm-power value)? Current evidence points to **tritium + materials**, because both gate the first plants regardless of physics success.
