## Expert forecasts

- **FIA 2025 survey**: companies split between "early 2030s" optimists and a large cluster expecting late-2030s/2040s for first grid electricity [S30]. 2026 survey grid-timing bins not yet extracted (pending).
- **IAEA World Fusion Outlook 2025**: company milestone targets span late-2020s to mid-2050s, clustering early-to-mid 2030s; 160+ devices worldwide [S32].
- **Skeptics**: Jassby (Bulletin, 2017; revisited Nov 2024) — high internal power consumption, no natural tritium, cost; argues fusion "is something to be shunned" [S35]. Ramana (UBC) — climate timelines miss. Common skeptic floor: material grid contribution 2050+.

## Central scenario (synthesis, 2026-10-02)

1. **Physics milestones land before economics**: Q>1 in a tokamak plausibly 2026–2028 (SPARC ~2027, BEST ~2027), building on NIF's ignition results.
2. **First grid electricity**: mid-2030s at the absolute earliest (CFS ARC early-2030s is the most aggressive *credible* corporate claim); industry median late-2030s/2040s; government roadmaps cluster ~2040 (ITER 2039, US pilot 2035–2040, STEP 2040s).
3. **Material grid contribution: 2050+** — which is the skeptics' core point about mid-century climate relevance.
4. **Binding constraints through the 2030s are engineering, not physics**: tritium self-sufficiency, materials data (IFMIF-DONES), heat exhaust, recirculating power; supply-chain chokepoints REBCO tape and Li-6 enrichment.
5. **Regulation is de-risking fastest** (US Part 30 ~2027, UK path, Canada consulting, first state license 2026).
