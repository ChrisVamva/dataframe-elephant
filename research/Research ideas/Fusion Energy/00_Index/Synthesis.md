# Synthesis — The Current State of Fusion Energy (October 2026)

_Answers to the five research questions, drawing on folders 01–05 and the findings log. Citations: `../06_Evidence/Sources.md`._

## RQ1 — Where is the state of the art?

The field crossed its most important physics thresholds. NIF has demonstrated ignition eleven times, with a record **target gain of 4.13** (8.6 MJ from 2.08 MJ of laser energy, April 2025) [S01]. Magnetic confinement holds its own record class: JET's **69 MJ D–T pulse** (Q≈0.33, 2023) remains the tokamak energy record [S04], while long-pulse operation has advanced dramatically — EAST ran H-mode plasma for **1,066 seconds** (Jan 2025) [S06] and W7-X set stellarator records (1.8 GJ over 360 s; 43 s triple-product, May 2025) [S08] [S09]. The crucial distinction: what has been demonstrated is *scientific* gain and endurance. No device has approached *engineering* breakeven — NIF's wall-plug gain is ~0.008 [S03], and the best tokamak Q is ~0.33. Q>1 in a tokamak is plausibly 1–2 years away (SPARC, BEST).

## RQ2 — Which approaches exist and how mature are they?

Six families, in descending order of maturity: **tokamaks** (deepest database; ITER, SPARC, STEP, BEST), **stellarators** (steady-state by design; W7-X validated the physics; fastest-rising private category — Proxima, Thea, Type One), **inertial confinement** (only demonstrated ignition; weakest power-plant story — efficiency, rep-rate, targets), then the alternates — **magnetized target** (General Fusion), **field-reversed configurations** (Helion, TAE), and **Z-pinches** (Zap) — which carry the most aggressive timelines with the least independent validation. Detail in `../02_Approaches/`.

## RQ3 — What have the major programs achieved?

ITER was rebaselined (research ops ~2033–34, D–T ~2039, +~€5B) but assembly is now running *ahead* of schedule — six of nine sector modules installed by Jul 2026 [S13] [S15]. China is building fast: BEST targets completion end-2027 for a burning-plasma demo [S16]. The UK committed >£2.5B and moved STEP into its delivery phase (grid power in the 2040s) [S18] [S19]. The US DOE issued a mid-2030s commercialization roadmap and funds a milestone-based private-program (8 companies) [S22] [S23]. In the private sector, **$14.24B cumulative investment across 56 companies** [S31] now backs ~10 credible ventures; flagship events of the last 18 months: CFS's $863M round with Nvidia/Google and 200 MW Google + >$1B Eni offtakes [S24], Helion's first private D–T operation [S25], General Fusion's Nasdaq listing after a near-death funding crisis [S29], and TAE's pending >$6B merger with Trump Media [S26].

## RQ4 — What are the principal obstacles?

Engineering, not physics. Ranked by how hard they gate the first plants: (1) **tritium fuel cycle** — no breeding blanket has ever operated in a fusion neutron environment; world tritium stock is tens of kg; Li-6 enrichment exists only in Russia and China [S49] [S33]; (2) **materials** — no 14 MeV-neutron-qualified structural material, and the qualification facility (IFMIF-DONES) won't deliver data before the mid-2030s; (3) **heat exhaust** — detached-divertor control at 10–20 MW/m²; (4) **economics** — early fusion is unlikely to be LCOE-competitive; the value case is firm power [S34]. Regulation, by contrast, is de-risking quickly (US Part 30, UK outside nuclear licensing, first US state license Sep 2026).

## RQ5 — What timelines are credible?

Announced dates range from Helion's 2028 Microsoft PPA (widely doubted; no public net gain) to EU-DEMO's ~2050. Credible synthesis: **Q>1 in a tokamak 2026–2028; first grid electricity mid-2030s at the absolute earliest, with the industry median in the late 2030s/2040s and government roadmaps clustering ~2040; material grid contribution 2050+** [S30] [S32] [S39]. The best single indicator set to watch: SPARC first plasma and Q>1, BEST completion (2027), and Helion's ability to disclose net gain before 2028.

## One-paragraph answer

Fusion in late 2026 is a field that has *finally demonstrated the physics it promised for seventy years* — ignition at NIF, sustained burning-plasma-class operation in tokamaks and stellarators — while the hard problem has visibly migrated from plasma physics to engineering, fuel cycle, and economics. Private capital ($4.5B in the last year alone) and real offtake contracts have created a plausible — though unproven — commercial race alongside the public programs, with the first genuine test of that race arriving when SPARC and BEST attempt Q>1 within the next two years, and the starkest open risk being Helion's 2028 delivery promise. Realistically: demonstration this decade, first plants in the 2030s–2040s, meaningful energy contribution after 2050.
