# Editing Process Protocol

## Overview

This protocol defines the standardized workflow for editing articles from initial draft through final publication. It synthesizes best practices from industry leaders (The Blue Garret, Journalism University) and ensures consistency, quality, and readability across all editorial work.

## Core Principles

1. **Be Human** – Edit with empathy; understand the author's voice and intent.
2. **Be Honest** – Address weaknesses the author may not have noticed.
3. **Be Kind** – Treat authors' work with respect; preserve their contributions.
4. **Be Optimistic** – Believe the underlying story is worth polishing.
5. **Be Inclusive** – Honor diverse perspectives and avoid bias.
6. **Be Respectful of the Reader** – Prioritize clarity, engagement, and accessibility.
7. **Be Collaborative** – Work closely with authors through comments and feedback loops.
8. **Be Focused** – Apply deliberate, layered editing rather than superficial fixes.

## The Three-Stage Revision Framework

### Stage 1: Big Picture Read (Structure & Purpose)
- **Goal:** Assess whether the article achieves its central purpose and maintains coherent organization.
- **Actions:**
  - Read the entire manuscript straight through without making changes.
  - Evaluate: Does the piece have a clear central idea? Does the opening engage? Does the conclusion satisfy?
  - Check logical flow: Beginning establishes context, middle develops the story, ending resolves threads.
  - Identify major structural issues (missing sections, redundant content, organizational gaps).
- **Output:** A revised outline and a list of high-level revision priorities.

### Stage 2: Structure, Flow, and Organization (Line Editing)
- **Goal:** Refine organization, transitions, and sentence-level clarity.
- **Actions:**
  - Review paragraph-level structure; ensure each section serves a distinct function.
  - Apply the **reverse outline** technique: write one-sentence summaries of each paragraph, then rearrange for optimal flow.
  - Check transitions between paragraphs; eliminate abrupt shifts.
  - Ensure the “string of pearls” structure (scenes, quotes, facts) maintains reader engagement.
  - Trim redundant or filler language; replace jargon with accessible terminology.
  - Read aloud to catch rhythm issues and awkward phrasing.
- **Output:** A cleaned manuscript with improved structure and tighter prose.

### Stage 3: Sentence-Level Line Editing (Polish)
- **Goal:** Eliminate remaining errors, enhance precision, and ensure professional polish.
- **Actions:**
  - Check grammar, punctuation, and spelling (use track changes for corrections).
  - Verify factual accuracy and proper attribution.
  - Confirm consistent tense, voice, and point of view.
  - Ensure consistent capitalization (titles, proper nouns, acronyms).
  - Replace vague or passive constructions with active, precise language.
  - Align tone with the target audience and publication style.
- **Output:** A fully polished manuscript ready for copyediting and proofreading.

## Key Editing Questions

After each stage, ask:
1. **Did I achieve my original intent?** Does the opening and closing reflect the same central idea?
2. **Is the organization logical?** Can an unfamiliar reader follow the sequence of ideas?
3. **Can any part be deleted?** Every paragraph must justify its presence.
4. **Should any section be reorganised?** Buried key revelations may damage engagement.
5. **Have I used clear, appropriate language?** Avoid jargon unless the audience requires it.
6. **Have I shown rather than told?** Replace abstract statements with vivid, concrete details.
7. **Would a non-specialist reader follow the argument?** External feedback confirms comprehension.

## Collaboration Workflow

1. **Initial Self-Edit** – Author revises draft independently after stepping away.
2. **First Pass Review** – Editor provides feedback via tracked changes and a summary of concerns.
3. **Second Pass** – Author incorporates feedback, focusing on the highest-priority issues.
4. **External Review** – Non-specialist reader evaluates clarity and engagement.
5. **Final Polish** – Editor performs line editing, then passes to copyeditor for grammar/spelling.
6. **Proofreading** – Final read-through for consistency, formatting, and typographical accuracy.

## Documentation & Tracking

- Maintain a **Story Spreadsheet** tracking revision history, comments, and decisions.
- Log all style-sheet updates (grammar rules, terminology, formatting preferences).
- Record external feedback received and how it influenced revisions.
- Store annotated versions in the project repository (e.g., `research/processed/`).

## Quality Assurance

- **Peer Review:** At least one secondary reviewer should approve before publication.
- **Consistency Check:** Verify that all headings, numbering, and citation formats match the publication style guide.
- **Accessibility:** Ensure alt-text for images, proper heading hierarchy, and readable font sizes.
- **Fact-Checking:** Confirm all statistics, quotes, and references are accurate and properly sourced.

## Timeline

| Phase | Estimated Duration |
|-------|-------------------|
| Big-Picture Read | 1–2 hours |
| Structure & Flow | 2–4 hours |
| Line Editing | 3–5 hours |
| Final Polish | 1–2 hours |
| Total | ~7–12 hours (depending on article length) |
