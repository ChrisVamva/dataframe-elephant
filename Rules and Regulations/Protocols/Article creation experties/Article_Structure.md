# Article Structure Protocol

## Status
Advisory companion to `ArticleCreation.md`, `Article_Techniques.md`, `Article_Circumstance.md`. Read-only against `notebooks/`; article outputs are derivative, not controlling.

---

## 1. Purpose
The evidence protocols govern *what* you can say. The circumstance protocol governs *how* it sounds. This protocol governs *how it's built* — the skeletal structure that carries the reader from hook to payoff without losing them. A feature article is not a report with better sentences; it's a narrative machine with specific moving parts in a specific order.

---

## 2. The Standard Feature Architecture (in order)

### 1. The Hook / Lead (1–4 paragraphs)
**Purpose:** Make the reader ask "what happens next?" or "how does this affect me?"
**Forms:**
- **Anecdotal/Scene:** A specific moment in a specific place with a specific person (you). *Best for consumer pieces.*
- **Descriptive:** A vivid picture of the problem landscape.
- **Startling Fact:** One verified number with stakes attached.
- **Question Lead:** Only if the question creates genuine tension, not curiosity-bait.
**Rule:** The lead earns the nut graf. If the reader doesn't hit the nut graf within ~150 words, the lead is too long.

### 2. The Nut Graf (1 paragraph)
**Purpose:** Tell the reader what this story is, why it matters to *them*, and what they'll get by the end.
**Formula:** Transition from lead + summary sentence answering a big-picture question (Why? How? So what?).
**Test:** Delete the lead. The nut graf alone must still state the story's promise.
**Placement:** Immediately after the lead block.

### 3. The Body — Organized as Sections, Not a Stream
**Purpose:** Deliver the evidence in digestible, progressive chunks that build toward the payoff.
**Structure options (pick ONE per article):**
- **Hourglass / Three-Act:** Wide (human scene) → Narrow (the evidence) → Wide (what it means). *Default for consumer pieces.*
- **Martini Glass:** Inverted-pyramid summary first (the finding in 3 sentences), then the narrative stem. *When the finding is actionable and readers may stop early.*
- **Number-Led:** One verified figure as spine; each section tests it from a new side. *When exactly one number carries the story.*
- **Audit / Trust Piece:** Gap counts as plot. *When the story is "what we don't know."*

**Section requirements (every section):**
- **Mini-nut:** One line opening the section saying what this chunk proves.
- **Scene → Data → Implication rhythm:** Never three data blocks without a human consequence between them.
- **Transition:** Explicit bridge from previous section (repeated word, logical connector, callback to the lead scene).
- **Inline evidence:** Claim meaning in prose; metric with unit/scope/class/confidence at first mention; "not recorded" for absences.

### 4. The "Key Number" Callout (if applicable)
**Purpose:** Isolate the single most important metric so it can't be missed.
**Form:** Boxed/sidebar callout — not a table. Bold number. Plain-language scope. What it means / what it doesn't mean.

### 5. The Gaps / Honesty Section
**Purpose:** Show the uncertainty *as practical guidance*, not academic hedging.
**Form:** "What we don't know, and why it matters to you." Each gap gets a "so what."

### 6. The Kicker / Closing
**Purpose:** Return the reader to the opening with changed understanding, or deliver the decision framework.
**Forms:**
- **Bookend:** Return to the lead scene/character with the problem resolved or reframed.
- **Solution/Possibilities:** Present the drills, the checklist, the questions to ask.
- **Call to Action:** Specific, reasonable, tied to the evidence.
**Rules:** No summary. No new claims. No bare quotes. The last line should be writable *before* drafting the body.

## 3. Transitions That Work

| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| **Repeated word/phrase** | "That silence is itself a buying signal." → Next section opens: "The silence shows up again when..." |
| **Logical connector** | "But here's the thing:" / "The trap is..." / "Good news:" / "The catch:" |
| **Callback to lead** | "Remember the hallway at 11:47 PM? Here's what the internet outage looks like there." |
| **Question bridge** | "So what about when the vendor pulls the plug?" |
| **Character bridge** | "The same switch that failed in Death 1? Here it works fine." |

---

## 4. Pre-Publication Structure Check

- [ ] Lead ≤ 4 paragraphs; nut graf within ~150 words
- [ ] Nut graf states: cluster in plain words, scale (cluster vs corpus), reader's promised decision
- [ ] Body uses ONE structural shape throughout (no mixing hourglass + martini)
- [ ] Every section has a mini-nut opener
- [ ] No three data blocks in a row without human consequence
- [ ] Transitions explicit between every section
- [ ] Key number called out (if applicable)
- [ ] Gaps framed as "what this means for you"
- [ ] Kicker returns to lead or delivers decision — no summary, no new claims
- [ ] Provenance panel in appendix; claim IDs out of main prose

## 5. Structural Templates by Article Type

| Article Type | Shape | Lead | Body Sections | Kicker |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer explainer | Hourglass | Scene (you in your house) | 3 failure modes, each: scene → evidence → drill | Bookend + checklist |
| Methods/trust piece | Audit | Startling gap count | Gap classes → why each matters → study proposal | The study proposal as payoff |
| Single-number story | Number-led | The number + stakes | Each section: new angle on the number | The number in context |
| Breaking/actionable | Martini Glass | 3-sentence summary finding | Narrative stem: how we got here, what it means | Immediate action steps |
