# Smart Home Market Research Prompt

Act as an independent market-research analyst. Produce a decision-useful,
evidence-grounded assessment of the smart-home market using the research
media and sources available in your current environment.

## Independence Requirement

This prompt is self-contained. Do not request, assume, or depend on access to
this checkout, any private repository, local database, workspace protocol,
script, hidden file, or environment variable. Do not ask the user to grant
access to such material. Do not include repository commands or repository
write-back steps in the deliverable.

If a search tool, database, report, or source is unavailable, state that
limitation and continue with accessible material. If no external search is
available, use any supplied documents or knowledge sources; clearly mark
time-sensitive claims as unverified and provide a prioritized source plan for
the next researcher. Never invent citations or market figures to fill a gap.

## Research Question

What is the current structure, direction, and opportunity landscape of the
residential smart-home market, and which segments, customer problems, and
business models are supported by the strongest accessible evidence?

The objective is market understanding and opportunity discovery, not an
investment recommendation or a claim that every connected-home segment is
attractive.

## Scope and Defaults

Use these defaults unless the user supplies alternatives. State every default
used at the beginning of the report.

- **Geography:** global, with country or regional differences called out; do
  not treat one country's evidence as globally representative.
- **Time period:** use the five most recent complete calendar years for
  historical evidence and a clearly labeled five-year forward outlook. State
  the actual years based on the execution date.
- **Market:** consumer/residential smart-home products and services, including
  connected devices, home control and automation, residential security,
  energy management, lighting, climate, entertainment, health/aging-related
  home applications, connectivity/hubs, voice or app control, installation,
  and relevant subscriptions.
- **Adjacent but distinct:** building-management systems for commercial
  property, general consumer electronics without meaningful home connectivity,
  utility-scale energy, and standalone telecom services. Include them only
  where they materially shape residential demand, competition, or infrastructure;
  label them as adjacent rather than blending them into the core market.
- **Audience:** a reader evaluating market structure, customer needs, and
  potential opportunities, without a predetermined product, company, or
  investment thesis.

If the question cannot be answered responsibly at global scope, state why and
present the best-supported regional view instead. Do not block on optional
clarifications; use the defaults and list the assumptions that matter.

## Research Method

1. Identify which research media are available: web search, browser, academic
   indexes, government or standards databases, company filings, market reports,
   product documentation, datasets, interviews, user-provided documents, or
   other accessible sources. Record what was actually used, not merely what
   could have been used.
2. Search across source types and geographies. Prioritize original evidence:
   regulatory and government sources, company filings and first-party
   documentation, standards bodies, peer-reviewed research, transparent
   surveys, and traceable datasets. Use reputable trade and general reporting
   to locate signals and corroborate developments. Treat vendor and paid
   market-research claims as interested evidence; report their scope and
   methods when available.
3. For every material claim, verify that the cited source supports the exact
   wording. Prefer independent corroboration for consequential market claims.
   Do not count sources repeating the same underlying report as independent
   confirmation.
4. Search for competing interpretations, non-adoption, product failures,
   privacy/security concerns, interoperability problems, and evidence that
   could weaken the leading conclusions.
5. Separate statements directly supported by a source from unverified
  indications, your analysis, and action recommendations. Label each claim or
  group clearly; do not let confident prose substitute for evidence.
6. Record the search date, relevant publication/data dates, search terms or
   databases where practical, source-selection method, and key limitations.

## Questions to Investigate

### Market boundaries and structure

- How do sources define “smart home,” “connected home,” and “home automation”?
- Which categories are included in market estimates, and which are excluded?
- How do device, platform, connectivity, installation, service, and
  subscription layers relate across the value chain?
- Which parts of the market are mature, emerging, fragmented, bundled, or
  dependent on a broader ecosystem?

### Demand, adoption, and customers

- What adoption or ownership evidence exists by country/region, household
  type, income, dwelling type, or other relevant segment?
- What jobs or problems lead people to adopt smart-home products: convenience,
  safety, energy cost/control, comfort, accessibility, health, entertainment,
  property management, or another need?
- What are the reported purchase triggers, willingness-to-pay signals,
  repeat-purchase patterns, retention/subscription behavior, and unmet needs?
- What prevents adoption or causes abandonment: cost, setup complexity,
  reliability, privacy, security, interoperability, fragmented apps, broadband
  access, landlord/tenant constraints, accessibility, or weak perceived value?
- Separate stated interest from observed purchasing, usage, retention, or
  willingness to pay.

### Technology and ecosystem

- What roles do devices, hubs/controllers, wireless protocols, home networks,
  cloud services, local control, mobile applications, voice assistants, and
  automation platforms play?
- How do interoperability initiatives and standards affect compatibility,
  switching costs, ecosystem control, and consumer experience? Verify current
  status and version/date rather than assuming a standard guarantees
  real-world compatibility.
- Which technical constraints materially affect adoption or business models:
  setup, uptime, latency, power, installation, lifecycle support, updates,
  cybersecurity, privacy, data portability, or service outages?
- Distinguish announced capabilities from independently observed or deployed
  capabilities.

### Competition and economics

- Map relevant device makers, platform/ecosystem owners, retailers,
  installers/integrators, utilities, telecom providers, security providers,
  property actors, and service businesses where evidence supports them.
- Compare selected competitors on target customer, use case, distribution,
  compatibility/ecosystem, price model, recurring revenue, support, and
  differentiation. Do not force false equivalence across different segments.
- Examine value capture, hardware margins where disclosed, subscriptions,
  installation/service revenue, bundling, channel incentives, and customer
  acquisition/retention evidence.
- Identify dependencies on platform access, app stores, cloud infrastructure,
  retailers, installers, standards, utilities, insurers, landlords, or
  regulation.

### Regulation, risk, and opportunity

- Identify relevant privacy, consumer-protection, cybersecurity, product
  safety, energy, accessibility, and communications requirements by
  jurisdiction where material. Cite the applicable primary authority and date;
  do not generalize one jurisdiction's law to another.
- Assess safety and security risks, privacy and surveillance concerns,
  unsupported devices, end-of-life policies, interoperability fragmentation,
  and household impacts.
- Identify evidence-backed unmet needs and underserved segments. For each
  opportunity, state the customer, problem, existing alternatives, evidence,
  business-model hypothesis, key dependency, and strongest falsifier.
- Rank opportunity hypotheses by evidence strength, customer significance,
  differentiation potential, accessibility of the segment, feasibility, and
  uncertainty. Treat scoring as a transparent comparison aid, not objective
  market truth.

## Market-Claim Rules

- Never present a market-size number without its source, publication/data
  year, geography, currency, segment definition, and method or stated
  limitation.
- Do not combine incompatible market estimates or sum overlapping categories.
  Explain differences in scope and methodology instead.
- Label forecasts as forecasts, vendor or analyst estimates as reported
  estimates, and your own calculations as calculations with inputs shown.
- Do not infer adoption from shipment estimates, stated interest, search
  volume, or a product launch unless the source supports that inference.
- Avoid unsupported precision. Prefer a bounded range and explain its basis
  when sources disagree.
- Preserve negative results, unavailable data, contradictory evidence, and
  uncertainty. Absence of a located source is not proof that a market or
  customer need does not exist.

## Required Deliverable

Write a self-contained report with these sections:

1. **Executive synthesis:** the strongest conclusions, important caveats, and
   two to five evidence-backed opportunity hypotheses.
2. **Scope and method:** defaults used, actual time period, research media
   accessed, search/screening approach, and limitations.
3. **Definitions and market boundaries:** what is and is not included, with
   conflicting definitions noted.
4. **Market structure and value chain:** segments, participants, dependencies,
   and how value flows.
5. **Demand and customer evidence:** adoption, needs, buying behavior,
   barriers, and segment variation.
6. **Technology and interoperability:** ecosystem map, current developments,
   and evidence-backed constraints.
7. **Competitive and business-model landscape:** representative alternatives,
   positioning, channels, and value capture.
8. **Regulation, risk, and failure modes:** jurisdiction and date specific.
9. **Opportunity hypotheses:** customer/problem/evidence/alternatives/model/
   dependency/falsifier for each.
10. **Uncertainty and counterevidence:** what could change the conclusions,
    what is disputed, and what could not be verified.
11. **Next research steps:** prioritized questions, target source types, and
    the observation that would resolve each question.
12. **Source register:** title, author or publisher, source type, URL or
    retrieval path, publication date, access date, geography/scope, and the
    claims supported. If a medium has no URL, identify it sufficiently for a
    reader to find it.

Include a claim-evidence table for the most consequential conclusions:

| Claim | Type (fact/signal/inference/recommendation) | Evidence and locator | Counterevidence | Date/scope | Confidence and reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Confidence describes evidence support, not market attractiveness:

- **High:** direct and relevant evidence, strong claim-source alignment, and
  appropriate corroboration for a material claim.
- **Medium:** relevant evidence exists but has limits in coverage,
  independence, recency, method, or representativeness; limits are explicit.
- **Low:** evidence is indirect, sparse, conflicting, interested, outdated, or
  mostly secondary; retain the claim only as a qualified lead or inference.

## Completion Check

Before submitting, verify that:

- the report answers the stated question and labels every important default;
- every material market claim is traceable and date/scope qualified;
- quoted or computed market estimates can be reconstructed from cited inputs;
- competing evidence, non-adoption, risks, and meaningful counterexamples
  were sought and reported;
- facts, signals, inferences, and recommendations are not conflated;
- inaccessible sources or tools are stated, with no fabricated substitute;
- no step requires access to a private repository, local path, database,
  script, or environment variable;
- the source register is sufficient for another researcher to verify the
  findings.

If evidence is too weak to answer part of the question, say so plainly and
return the most useful supported findings plus a concrete next-research plan.
