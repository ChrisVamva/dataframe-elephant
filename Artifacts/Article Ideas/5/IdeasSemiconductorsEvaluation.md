## Rating method

Five criteria, 1–5 each, total /25 (house method, per `Artifacts/Article Ideas/2/Ratings/Best5.md`). Scored from the idea entry plus its cited register entries — no new evidence introduced.

| Criterion | 5 means | 1 means |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (H) | broad reader appeal, memorable takeaway | niche/methods-only interest |
| Evidence (E) | dense verified register ids + official/primary backing | thin, single-source, or estimate-dependent backing |
| Novelty (N) | says something the current coverage does not | repeats commonplace trade-press framing |
| Feasibility (F) | writable from the Wave 5 corpus as-is | needs primary research or resolution of open follow-ups first |
| Integrity (I) | turns limits into the story, hard to overclaim | easy to misread, overclaim, or date rapidly |

Tie-breaks, in order: broader audience > synthesis value across notes > drafting readiness (evidence already verified).

## Why the top 5 win — the reasoning before the list

- **1.1 wins on completeness.** It is the only idea with a verified numbered hook ($4.3B), a three-jurisdiction data series (S24), a mechanism (refinery pools, S40), and an open-questions coda (Armenia re-export, S35) that functions as part of the plot. Integrity 5: the customs-data basis and its limits are already in the register.
- **5.1 is the wave's thesis.** The transformation-ledger sketch is the only idea that synthesizes every folder (regimes → cases → technologies → design). N5 because nothing in current coverage proposes append-not-overwrite as the laundering fix. E5 because each design choice lands on a verified precedent (S48/S50/S23/S28); its "weakness" — it's a design, not a report — is disclosed, hence I5.
- **1.3 converts an official dashboard into a story.** The 86% reversal (S20) is verified, recent, and counter-intuitive; docked I4 because detention statistics measure enforcement attention as much as crime — the companion piece 8.3 exists to carry that caveat.
- **3.1 has the densest primary evidence in the wave** (IEA 2025+2026 fetched, USGS chapters, P-class throughout). Docked N4 — refinery-concentration pieces exist — but none pairs "where 99% of gallium comes from" with "where 100% of provenance dies" in the same building.
- **2.1 beats several flashier ideas on durability.** Both regulatory texts were fetched in full (P-class); the comparison is structural, not news-driven, and stays valid until Dec 2027. H3 is honest — trade-policy audiences only — but F5/I5: nothing can date it except amendent.

**Cut line reasoning:** the next tier (8.1, 4.1, 1.2, all 23) are commissioning-ready reference or narrative pieces — 8.1 and 8.2 work best as linked companions to the winners rather than leads (methods-only hook), 4.1 and 1.2 green-light well once flagships are underway.
