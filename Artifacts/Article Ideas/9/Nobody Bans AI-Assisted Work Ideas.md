## Rating method — the two requested axes

Each idea gets two 1–5 scores, total **/10**:

| Axis | 5 means | 1 means |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence support (E)** — how well our claims are backed by the sources today | every material claim carries a verbatim first-party quote or P-class anchor in S01–S31; absence claims scoped to a named, fully-fetched document with stated search design | claims would outrun the register: Blocked/C-class sources carrying the load, scope creep beyond the fetch design, open follow-ups unresolved |
| **Hook (H)** — attractiveness of reading: headline, opening scene, takeaway | specific, ironic or emotional lead a general reader follows; a quotable one-line takeaway | methods-forward or analytical; interest confined to a specialist beat |

**Tie-breaks, in order:** (1) higher total → (2) **higher E** (at equal totals, the better-evidenced idea leads: it carries the wave's central claims with the least overclaim risk) → (3) higher H → (4) synthesis value across the wave's notes, then drafting readiness (open rechecks rank last). Scores are integers; the tie-break log below fixes within-group order.

**How E was scored in this register.** The audit is quote-rich — Upwork §3.2, Fiverr's Community Standards and guidelines, Steam/Etsy/FTC/EU text are verbatim in hand `[S01, S03, S04, S21–S25]` — which lifts most takes to E5. E drops to 4 where a load-bearing element is **Blocked** (Fiverr ToS body `[S06]`), an **absence claim presses beyond its fetch scope** (industry-wide framing), or **C-class research carries a quantitative headline** `[S26]`. Floor is E4: central evidence is first-party documents, not estimates.

## The ideas

### NB-01 — The Contract for Robots and the Clause for Humans
- **Take:** Upwork gives autonomous AI agents a first-class contract — AI Agent Playground terms where agents fetch job posts and submit *unedited* deliverables, and feedback "will constitute the sole form of payment" `[S02]` — while a *human's* AI-assisted proposal is misconduct only "if such use is restricted by your client" `[S01]`. Same platform, two rulebooks: AI-as-participant regulated in detail; AI-as-method left to the client's whim. Open by reading the two texts side by side.
- **Anchors:** S01 (§3.2 + honesty clause, verbatim), S02 (Playground terms v1.2, eff. 2025-07-29, verbatim).
- **Scores: E5 + H5 = 10** — both instruments first-party, fetched, quoted in full; the irony *is* the headline. **Caveat:** stick to Upwork's public text; no claims about intent or internal policy.

### NB-02 — The Rule Nobody Wrote
- **Take:** The wave's spine as a feature piece: audit all 12 platforms' public governing documents on one day — **0 ban AI-assisted work, 1 allows it, 6 say nothing** `[S01–S19]` — while every platform markets AI categories `[S30]`, Upwork reports a +34% hourly premium for AI work `[S26]`, and peer-reviewed evidence shows a substitution shock in writing/translation `[S28]`. The freelance AI economy runs on a rule nobody wrote. Ends with the model clause.
- **Anchors:** full register; counts from `02_Platform_Policy_Audit/` §1; demand from S26/S28; deliverable from `05_Clause_Design/`.
- **Scores: E5 + H4 = 9** — counts mechanically verifiable against fetched documents; H4 because a survey-frame opening needs NB-01/NB-06's contradictions to hook general readers. **Caveat:** always say "public documents, fetched 2026-10-16".

### NB-03 — The Client Is the Regulator
- **Take:** In the vacuum, platforms outsourced AI policy to the buyer: Upwork makes AI use misconduct only where "such use is restricted by your client" `[S01]`; Fiverr makes it a pre-order negotiation the client must raise "before placing the order or immediately after" `[S04]`. Result: **the same platform has different AI rules on every contract**, and compliance depends on who you happen to be negotiating with. Delegated governance: the platform keeps discretion, the client legislates, the freelancer absorbs both.
- **Anchors:** S01 (conditional trigger), S04 (opt-out timing + dispute factors), S03 (backstop: "Misrepresenting the use of AI tools is a breach of trust").
- **Scores: E5 + H4 = 9** — both operative clauses quoted first-party; H4: strong thesis ("nobody banned it — they made it everyone else's problem") that wants a concrete buyer/seller scene to open hard. **Caveat:** no dispute anecdotes exist — enforcement data is absent from every document.

### NB-04 — What Steam, Etsy and Brussels Know About AI Work That Upwork Doesn't
- **Take:** The rule isn't hard to write: Steam requires declaring pre-generated and live-generated AI content and reviews it "the same way we evaluate all non-AI content," with mere efficiency tooling out of scope `[S21]`; Etsy mandates one listing field `[S22]`; the FTC ruled "AI-generated reviews are covered by the final rule" `[S24]`; EU AI Act Art. 50 requires machine-readable marking `[S25]`. One operable pattern — **allow, declare, own the output, punish harms** — exists four times over outside labour platforms. Close with the transfer: nine elements + model clause.
- **Anchors:** S21, S22, S23, S24, S25 (all P-class, verbatim); bridge to `05_Clause_Design/ClauseDesign.md`.
- **Scores: E5 + H4 = 9** — every comparator primary and quotable; H4: constructive pieces hook less than exposés, but "the rule already exists — four times" is a clean takeaway. **Caveat:** drafting precedents only; never imply these laws govern freelance contracts.

### NB-05 — The Agent Is Already a Party to the Contract
- **Take:** Forward-looking: the hardest clause in platform law is "who does the work." Upwork's honesty clause already polices it for humans — you can't "falsely claim one freelancer will do a job when another will actually do it" `[S01]` — while its Playground terms let a machine be the performer `[S02]`. As agent-submitted work spreads, every silent platform `[S07, S09, S12–S14, S19]` inherits a contract drafted on the assumption a person types the deliverable.
- **Anchors:** S01 (honesty clause), S02 (agent deliverables), S04 (Fiverr: accountable "regardless of the use of AI tools"), silent-platform rows.
- **Scores: E5 + H4 = 9** — two verbatim instruments anchor it; H4: the freshest forward frame in the bank. **Caveat:** the *spread* of agent-submitted work is `[inference]` — no platform discloses agent volumes. Redundancy: sequel to NB-01; do not run back-to-back.

### NB-06 — The Number That Doesn't Exist
- **Take:** Buyers could settle this debate with one figure — the AI-assisted share of delivered work — and **no audited platform publishes it** (absence across all fetched documents, 2026-10-16). What exists pulls opposite ways: +34% hourly premium for AI work `[S26]` (C-class, self-reported) against peer-reviewed −20–50% demand collapse in substitutable skills and declining novice demand `[S28]`. The story is the void itself: platforms profit on both sides of an information asymmetry — selling AI categories `[S30]` while withholding the disclosure that would ground buyer trust.
- **Anchors:** scoped absence across S01–S20, S28 (P anchor), S26 (C, framed as company claim), S30.
- **Scores: E4 + H5 = 9** — title-level hook; E4 because the absence claim must stay scoped to the audit's fetch design, and the strongest quantitative line is C-class. **Caveat:** publish the scope sentence ("none of the twelve audited platforms' public documents") or the claim outruns its evidence.

### NB-07 — The AI Rule You Can't Find in the Contract
- **Take:** The one platform that wrote AI rules put them everywhere *except* the document you'd check: Fiverr's AI policy lives in its Community Standards ("Fiverr embraces new technology and allows..." `[S03]`) plus two Help Center articles with the operational detail `[S04, S05]` — while the ToS body itself was **unretrievable** (client-rendered; archive captures hold only page shells) `[S06]`. Governed by documents that aren't in the contract and can't be archived, the platform's binding hierarchy is exactly what a lawyer, journalist, or auditor would want to check — and can't.
- **Anchors:** S03, S04, S05 (rule text, verbatim), S06 (Blocked, with fetch/archive trail), plus the four-layer stack in `01_Background/Concepts.md` §2.
- **Scores: E4 + H5 = 9** — the *existence and location* of the rules is fully evidenced; E4 because "not in the ToS" cannot be verified while the ToS is Blocked — the piece must be framed as "the rule we could verify lives outside the contract." **Caveat:** never assert the ToS *lacks* an AI clause; assert we could not read it, and that the guidance points to Community Standards.

### NB-08 — Platforms Sell AI, Regulate It Last
- **Take:** The positioning gap as a market story: all 12 platforms merchandise AI (category trees, "AI Gig Work", trending skills) `[S30]`; 1 of 12 regulates worker AI use `[S03, S04]`. Meanwhile Upwork brands itself "the world's human and AI-powered work marketplace" `[S26]` and independent research shows the demand mix already restructured `[S28]`. Marketing moved first; the rulebook never caught up — and the article can show the ordering in one table.
- **Anchors:** S30 (site observations), S26/S27 (corporate framing, C-class), S28 (P anchor), audit counts.
- **Scores: E4 + H4 = 8** — taxonomy observations are solid first-party but evidence *structure*, not volume; the demand lines mix C- and P-class. H4: broad relevance, but "companies market faster than they govern" is a familiar shape — it needs the audit table to feel new. **Caveat:** category trees never prove demand size.

### NB-09 — Terms Written Before ChatGPT
- **Take:** A datable-document method piece: platform governance carries visible timestamps, and some of them predate the thing they're supposed to regulate — freelancermap's Terms are a "Conditions State December 2018" document with zero AI provisions `[S12]`, while Arc and Codementor both last modified 2026-08-31 with the same silence `[S13, S14]`. Reading a platform's AI maturity by the date on its terms is a replicable test readers can run themselves on any platform.
- **Anchors:** S12 (Dec-2018 state, verbatim), S13, S14 (modification dates, verbatim), method in `01_Background/Concepts.md` §5.
- **Scores: E4 + H3 = 7** — date lines are verbatim, but the implication ("hasn't been revised for AI") is `[inference]` with an open recheck (a newer revision page may exist — verification queue). H3: a methods-forward read with a checklist hook. **Caveat:** frame as "the document we fetched is dated 2018," not "the platform hasn't updated since."

### NB-10 — An AI-Only Marketplace With No AI Rules
- **Take:** The Botpool paradox `[S19, S20]`: a marketplace built entirely for AI services — vetted AI categories, AI-tool resale, "AI-native matching" — has short terms (15 sections) with **no AI-work clause at all**. Being AI-native did not produce AI-work governance, which kills the natural counterargument that new entrants will fix the silence the incumbents left. Small, ironic, and a perfect sidebar in NB-02 — or a tight standalone for the niche-market beat.
- **Anchors:** S19 (full terms read — the claim rests on a complete small document), S20 (positioning, C-class).
- **Scores: E5 + H3 = 8** — the whole document was read; the absence claim is as strong as this method allows. H3: the irony lands for a market/design audience, but the corpus is one short document — thin as a full feature. **Caveat:** pair with the second FRE niche board when it is profiled (open follow-up in the FRE scans).

### NB-11 — Policies You Can't Read Without a Browser
- **Take:** The methods story with a antagonist: platform governance is increasingly *client-rendered* — Fiverr's legal pages serve shell HTML to plain fetches, and Wayback captures (2023, 2024, 2026) preserve the shell and catalogue JSON but not the terms text `[S06]`. Archive.org cannot preserve what browsers must execute. An independent researcher auditing platform rules on 2026-10-16 hit 403s, SPAs, and rate limits across the web (documented in Wave 8 Findings, method limits) — the rules are public but not *inspectable*, which is a new kind of opacity.
- **Anchors:** S06 (fetch/archive evidence trail), Findings method limits (tooling transparency), FRE's `Blocked` discipline `[S31]`.
- **Scores: E5 + H3 = 8** — the evidence is first-hand fetch logs and archived shells we hold; H3: genuinely novel but methods-flavoured — best as the closing section of NB-07 than a lead. **Caveat:** no claims about *why* pages are client-rendered (architecture, not motive).

### NB-12 — Fraud Law Is Doing AI's Job
- **Take:** The analytical take: with no AI clause, ordinary misconduct provisions absorb AI disputes case-by-case — misrepresentation `[S01, S15]`, IP/DMCA `[S01, S19]`, fake-review bans now overlaid by the FTC's rule that "AI-generated reviews are covered" `[S24]`. What this default reaches (fake identity, stolen work) and what it cannot (competent, undisclosed, client-never-restricted AI delivery) defines the real legal perimeter of freelance AI today — and why Fiverr's "free from clear mistakes or AI-generated hallucinations" quality line `[S04]` matters so much.
- **Anchors:** S01, S15, S19 (fallback clauses), S24 (FTC), S03/S04 (quality floor as the missing middle), inference labeled per Findings RQ1.
- **Scores: E4 + H3 = 7** — every clause is quoted, but the *synthesis* (default reaches X, not Y) is `[inference]` doing the heavy lifting; H3: legal-analytical opening, hard to scene. **Caveat:** no jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction claims; the piece is about contract defaults, not law's outer bounds.

### NB-13 — The Enforcement Ladder Nobody Climbs Publicly
- **Take:** Every stated sanction exists on paper and nowhere in public data: Fiverr publishes a ladder (order cancellation → full refund → permanent suspension) with no counts, cases, or appeal outcomes `[S04]`; no audited platform publishes any AI-related enforcement statistic (absence, 2026-10-16). Deterrence in the freelance AI market rests entirely on unobservable sanctions — an accountability piece built around what platforms *could* do versus what they will say they did.
- **Anchors:** S04 (ladder, verbatim), scoped absence across S01–S20, FFWI context S26.
- **Scores: E4 + H3 = 7** — the ladder is quoted and the absence scoped, but a published-stats piece needs a target: outreach to platforms would be required to move past the document audit (feasibility drag reflected in E). **Caveat:** silence ≠ non-enforcement; the piece must say "unpublished," never "unenforced."

## Limitations

- **Absence claims are fetch-scoped:** the E4 scores on NB-06, NB-07, NB-08, NB-09, NB-12 and NB-13 reflect that pressing "no platform publishes X" beyond the twelve audited public documents outruns the method (Gate E, `Research-Evaluation.md`).
- **One Blocked source shapes two takes:** Fiverr's ToS body `[S06]` caps NB-07's evidence and must never be asserted as *absence* of a clause.
- **C-class quantitative lines** (Upwork FFWI, `[S26]`) are self-reported; they carry hooks in NB-06 and NB-08 and must stay labeled as company research.
- **No enforcement reality-check exists:** NB-12 and NB-13 describe stated powers and public silence — never observed practice.
- **Snapshot dating:** all scores assume the register of 2026-10-16; a terms revision anywhere re-rates NB-02's counts first.
