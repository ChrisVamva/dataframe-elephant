## Rating method

Five criteria, 1–5 each, total /25. Scored from the idea entry plus its cited register entries — no new research introduced.

| Criterion | 5 means | 1 means |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (H) | broad reader appeal, memorable takeaway | niche/methods-only interest |
| Evidence (E) | dense verified register ids + official/primary backing | thin, single-source, or estimate-dependent backing |
| Novelty (N) | says something current coverage does not | repeats commonplace trade-press framing |
| Feasibility (F) | writable from the Wave 7 corpus as-is | needs primary research or resolution of open follow-ups first |
| Integrity (I) | turns limits into the story, hard to overclaim | easy to misread, overclaim, or date rapidly |

Tie-breaks, in order: **broader audience > synthesis value across notes > drafting readiness** (evidence already verified).

**How Wave 7's evidence classes were scored.** Product-documentation claims (the survey's group B/C/H/I rows, S17–S47, S81–S103) are all **C** — the vendor's own docs are the only public description of its memory — so a pitch whose evidence plan is vendor-docs-only caps at E4 even when dense. P-class anchors (academic preprints S01–S16, S65–S80; regulators S58–S63; standards S33–S34) lift E to 5. Reliability was applied as labelled: vendor benchmark figures (S15, S16, S48, S54, S56) treated as self-reported and non-comparable; 2026-dated security preprints (S67–S72, S77–S80) treated as paper-reported and not independently replicated; the Council of Europe instrument (S64) as a draft located via secondary reporting; unresolved items (Agentforce, Cursor Projects persistence, real-world incident rates) as open follow-ups, never as facts.

## Why the top 5 win — the reasoning before the list

- **No tier owns the top of the table.** The five winners span the survey's scope deliberately: the umbrella privacy framing (1.3, background), the personal-agent tier's inversion (4.1), the taxonomy (1.1), the context-literature myth-bust (1.2), and the self-hosting security counterpoint (4.3). No two winners draw on the same evidence backbone, so the commission has zero redundant verification.
- **Every winner is evidence-dense with mixed reliability.** Each top-5 pitch carries P-class anchors alongside its C-class product docs, so no winner rests on vendor claims alone. The two H5 hooks in the wave (1.3, 3.1) split: 1.3 wins its tier on breadth of evidence, 3.1 loses its group on C-only evidence and press-covered novelty.
- **The I scores are all 5s by design.** The ideas file's research guardrails (vendor-claim status, unreplicated preprints, draft law, no real-world incident data) were written into the pitches themselves, so the highest-scoring ideas are also the hardest to overclaim. The one I4 in the 22-group (5.1) reflects real sensationalism risk — lab numbers reported as if they were incidents.
- **The cut line falls at a cliff, not a gradient.** The top five hold 22–23; the next tier holds 21. The three 21s are strong but interchangeable with each other, which is the definition of a commissioning queue, not a lead list.

## Limitations

- **Vendor-documentation bias:** nearly every product claim in the survey is a C source — the vendor's own docs are the only public description of its memory. Evidence scores treat this accurately (dense C-only plans cap at E4), but several pitches' F/I scores would improve with one reproducible first-hand test per product, which this corpus does not contain.
- **2026-dated security preprints (S67–S72, S77–S80):** figures are paper-reported and not independently replicated here. They carry the E5 clusters behind 4.3, 5.1, 5.2, 5.3, and 6.3 — replication would move those scores.
- **Vendor benchmarks (S15, S16, S48, S54, S56):** self-reported and non-comparable; they limit the E and I ceilings of 7.2 and 6.3 by construction.
- **Draft law (S64):** located via secondary reporting only; drives the F3/I4 on 3.2 and 6.2.
- **No real-world incident data:** no disclosed memory-poisoning or cross-tenant-leakage incident exists; every security claim is lab/benchmark. This is why 5.1 carries I4.
- **Single-observation field note** (Kiro/Devin/Cursor, `06_Evidence/Findings.md` RQ3): treated as a lead, not evidence; it slightly tempers 2.1's F.
- **Scale granularity:** 1–5 integer scores compress fine differences; the tie-break log, not the totals alone, carries the ordering within groups.
